Abuse is never a private matter.

Safe Harbor explains what violence against women and their children looks like, what Philippine law says about it, and where to find help.

Get help now Check the warning signs

Three things to know

It is a crime. Republic Act 9262, the Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act of 2004, makes violence against women and their children by a husband, ex-husband, partner, ex-partner or dating partner punishable by law.[1]

It is more than physical. The law also covers sexual, psychological and economic abuse, including threats, harassment, stalking and controlling a woman's money.[1]

Anyone can help. The law treats violence against women and children as a public crime, so a person who has personal knowledge of what happened may start a complaint, not only the victim.[1]
