Your Legal Protection

RA 9262 and the three kinds of protection orders, in plain language.

RA 9262 in plain language

Republic Act 9262, the Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act of 2004, makes VAWC a crime and gives women and children a way to ask for immediate protection.[1]

This page explains the law in simple terms for awareness. It is not legal advice. For advice about a real situation, talk to a lawyer, a barangay or police women and children protection desk, or the Public Attorney's Office.[13]

Three kinds of protection orders

A protection order is an order to stop the abuser from committing further violence. Choose a tab to compare them.[1]

Barangay Protection Order

Who issues it
The Punong Barangay. If the Punong Barangay is not available, a barangay kagawad may issue it, with a written statement explaining why.
How fast
On the day the application is filed.
How long
15 days.
What it does
Orders the abuser to stop committing acts of physical harm or threats of physical harm against the woman or her child.

It is the quickest and nearest option because it is handled at the barangay hall.[1]

Temporary Protection Order

Who issues it
The court.
How fast
On the date the application is filed, after the court decides it should be issued.
How long
30 days. If the hearing cannot be held in time, the court renews it for 30 days at a time until a final decision.
What it can do
The court may grant any or all of the reliefs in the law, such as ordering the abuser to stay away, giving temporary custody of children, and ordering financial support.

A TPO also sets the date of the hearing on whether to issue a permanent order.[1]

Permanent Protection Order

Who issues it
The court.
How
After notice and a hearing.
How long
It does not have a fixed 15 or 30 day limit like the BPO and TPO.

Protection orders are enforced by law enforcement agencies.[1]

Who can ask for help from the law

The woman or child who was harmed can apply for a protection order. The law also lets other people file the petition for her, including her parents or guardians, close relatives, a social worker, a police officer, a barangay official, a lawyer, a counselor, a therapist, a health care provider, or at least two concerned and responsible citizens of the city or town where the violence took place.[1]

Because VAWC is a public crime, any citizen who has personal knowledge of what happened may start the complaint.[1]

No pressure to "make peace"

A barangay official or a court hearing an application for a protection order may not order, force or unduly influence the woman to compromise or to give up what she is asking for.[1]

Other laws that connect to VAWC

  • RA 9710, Magna Carta of Women: recognizes and protects women's rights, including their right to be free from violence.[9]
  • RA 7610: protects children against abuse, exploitation and discrimination.[10]
  • RA 8353, Anti-Rape Law of 1997: classifies rape as a crime against persons.[11]
  • RA 11313, Safe Spaces Act: addresses gender-based sexual harassment in streets, public spaces, online spaces, schools and workplaces.[12]

Free legal help

The Public Attorney's Office (PAO) gives free legal assistance to people who qualify.[13]
