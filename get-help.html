Where to Get Help

Hotlines, desks and offices you can contact, and how to support someone else.

If you or your children are in danger right now

Call 911. If you cannot speak, you can still stay on the line. Go to a place where there are other people, if you can do so safely.[4]

Who to contact

Use the filters to narrow the list. Numbers can change, so check the source before you rely on one.

  • National Emergency Hotline

    911

    For immediate danger. DSWD lists 911 as a way to report VAWC.[4]

    Emergency

  • Aleng Pulis Hotline (PNP Women and Children Protection Center)

    0919-777-7377 or 0966-725-5961

    Police reporting line for violence against women and children. The PNP Women and Children Protection Center also lists this mobile number for reporting emergency cases.[4][6]

  • Barangay VAW Desk

    At your barangay hall

    Ask for the Violence Against Women (VAW) Desk. This is where you can apply for a Barangay Protection Order, which can be issued on the day you file.[1][4]

  • PNP Women and Children Protection Desk

    At the nearest police station

    Report abuse, ask for help with a complaint, and get referred to other services.[4]

  • DSWD and your city or municipal social welfare office

    Ask for the nearest DSWD field office or your local social welfare office

    DSWD runs centers and residential facilities that give temporary shelter and protective custody to women and children who need special protection.[5]

Steps that can help you stay safer

  • Tell at least one person you trust what is happening.
  • Keep a record of what happens: dates, photos of injuries, messages and receipts. Keep copies somewhere the abuser cannot reach, such as with a trusted person.
  • Keep your IDs, important papers, some money and your phone charger where you can get them quickly.
  • Ask your barangay about a Barangay Protection Order. It can be issued on the day you apply.[1]
  • Remember that you do not need to decide everything today. Contacting one place is a start.

Helping a friend

If someone you know may be in an abusive relationship, how you respond matters. Tap each point to read more.

Listen and believe them

Let them talk without interrupting. Say that you believe them and that it is not their fault.

Do not blame or push

Avoid asking "Why don't you just leave?" or telling them what to do. They know their situation and the risks best. Respect their pace.

Do not confront the abuser

Facing the abuser yourself can put both of you at risk. Let the barangay, police or other authorities handle it.

Offer something specific

Offer to go with them to the barangay VAW desk or police desk, help them find the numbers above, mind the children for an hour, or keep copies of important papers.

Know that you can report

VAWC is a public crime. A person with personal knowledge of what happened may start the complaint, and the law also allows concerned citizens to file for a protection order. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.[1]
